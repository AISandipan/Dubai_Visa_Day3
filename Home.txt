Time-series analytics and forecasting are essential for predicting future sales and revenue by analyzing historical data points collected at regular intervals (e.g., daily, monthly, quarterly).

-------------------------------------------------------
Key Components of Time-Series Data
Trend: The long-term direction of the data (upward, downward, or flat).
Seasonality: Repeating, predictable patterns within a specific timeframe (e.g., holiday sales spikes).
Cyclical Patterns: Longer-term, non-regular fluctuations driven by economic cycles.
Noise (Irregular/Random): Random, unpredictable variations that do not follow a pattern.

Common Forecasting Techniques
Moving Averages (MA): Smooths out short-term fluctuations to identify underlying trends by averaging consecutive data segments.
Exponential Smoothing: A weighted average approach that gives more importance to recent data, making it useful for volatile sales data.
ARIMA (AutoRegressive Integrated Moving Average): A robust model that combines autoregression (past values), differencing (to make data stationary), and moving averages to forecast.
Regression Analysis: Identifies relationships between sales and external factors (e.g., marketing spend, pricing)
